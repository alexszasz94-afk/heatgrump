## What the benchmark supports

- **Believable creator presentation:** store examples show ordinary shopper/creator framing, everyday clothes, visible faces and expressions, and a product or drink held in the scene. They are useful references for camera presence rather than posed fashion campaigns.
- **Clear light and visible subjects:** well-exposed retail aisles and warmly lit homes keep the face, hands and product readable. Natural-looking does not mean dim, noisy or muddy.
- **Useful background depth:** shelving, aisles, doorways and home furnishings establish a real setting while preserving a clear subject area. Clean composition can include merchandise or lived-in detail; it need not be an empty room.
- **Readable reactions and hooks:** annotations repeatedly highlight reaction, visual quality, lighting and realistic-looking avatars. Some call out a pattern interruption, a store discovery, gifting, or a 3-2-1 reveal. Identify the source clip's hook and preserve its timing rather than inventing a new concept during recasting.
- **Simple staging:** gift scenes use recognizable home spaces and a clear giver/recipient relationship; blanket reveals use visible people and cloth in a store. Judge new locations by whether the actual performance would look plausible there.
- **Transitions and sound matter:** the board praises music and simple transitions. Those are author assessments until the linked moving examples are checked. Still-image selection alone cannot verify them.

Several screenshots display engagement labels: examples inspected include 104K likes / 5 days ago and 29.9K likes / 4 days ago in the store group, and 16.3K likes in a gifting example. These are historical numbers printed in screenshots, not live verified analytics or proof that a background caused performance. Treat the author's causal comments as hypotheses.

## Explicit sourcing preference

Esteban asked to take the avatars and backgrounds from Pinterest and rejected generated substitutes. Use the actual selected Pinterest photographs as references. Do not silently generate a new person, an environment sheet, or a glossy approximation. Follow a later explicit request to generate assets if he changes this preference.

Pinterest origin alone is not a quality test. Compare each actual candidate with the benchmark for creator framing, lighting, face detail, setting and scene geometry. Keep source attribution and unresolved rights/provenance factual.

## Corrections learned from the rejected recast board

The user rejected both the generated board and the subsequent weak photo selections. For this candid retail/UGC direction, a posed fashion collage is a poor default avatar even when its navy sweater matches. A dim industrial roll-storage aisle is a poor substitute for a clear contemporary shopping aisle. A weathered spectator stand or dated car interior should not pass merely because the source also contains a stadium or car. Select for the complete visual brief and current source shot, not the location noun.

Lead the next review with a strong source-frame / avatar / background comparison that demonstrates this standard. Do not rehabilitate the rejected candidates through new labels or a better-looking document. Their earlier media IDs and assets are historical rejects unless the user explicitly selects them again.
