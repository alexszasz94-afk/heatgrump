# Pinterest research and visual review board

## Research method

Start from the user's benchmark, not a generic mood-board theme. Inspect several representative examples at readable size and the nearby annotations. Capture observable qualities (light direction, face detail, eye-level camera, shopping context, foreground clearance) separately from unverified interpretations ("this performed because..."). A board can establish taste even when its linked videos or performance numbers have not been verified. Do not claim to have watched videos from still screenshots.

Use available browser or search tools; discover their supported interfaces rather than assuming a Pinterest API exists. Prefer direct pin/source inspection to search snippets. Pinterest account access may vary. If login or availability prevents inspection, report that limitation and use public Pinterest results or another source with clear attribution; never describe a substitute as Pinterest research completed.

Build queries around observed product use and source geometry. Examples are hypotheses, not fixed product stereotypes:

| Source requirement | Useful search direction |
|---|---|
| Skincare applied over a counter | Bathroom vanity, daylight, clear counter, matching mirror placement |
| Drink poured in close-up | Kitchen or cafe worktop, compatible cup space and camera height |
| Fitness product used standing | Home gym or studio with full-body clearance and visible floor |
| Device demonstrated while seated | Desk setup with matching screen position and hand clearance |

Derive searches from the benchmark plus physical requirements. For example, a bright shopper reaction in a home-goods aisle calls for contemporary retail photography with visible floor, eye-level depth and room for a presenter, not any photograph tagged "warehouse." Evaluate promising results before expanding the shortlist. If the results share a mismatch, change the query or visual direction rather than accepting the first category match or collecting more of the same.

For avatars, search creator presentation, framing, wardrobe and use context. A candid shopper or creator still and an editorial fashion pose serve different purposes. Inspect face size, obstruction, pose, skin texture, eye detail and usable body coverage; a collage with a small obscured face may be an outfit reference but a poor identity input. Avoid guessing personality, health, ethnicity, or other sensitive traits from photos. Use the user's requested audience or casting brief when provided. “Trustworthy-looking” is not evidence of credibility.

## Select images that meet the brief

Assess each candidate as strong, usable with a specific limitation, or reject on the following dimensions. This is a comparative creative judgment, not a measured predictor of performance.

| Dimension | What to inspect |
|---|---|
| Benchmark match | Does the actual photograph have the user's desired creator presentation, light and composition? |
| Usable image quality | Is the subject/space clear at the intended reference size, without heavy filters, compression or distracting artifacts? |
| Avatar readability | Are the face and relevant body regions visible, with suitable angle and expression range for the performance? |
| Background composition | Is there coherent depth and space for the product/person, with manageable clutter and a useful foreground? |
| Physical compatibility | Do floor, support surfaces, viewpoint, scale, obstacles and light work with the source action? |
| Product and hook fit | Does the setting make the product use and reaction legible rather than distracting from them? |

Reject a candidate when a core requirement fails; do not average away an unreadable face or impossible contact geometry with attractive colors. Explain any accepted limitation concretely. Prefer a clearly seen suitable photograph over an uninspected search thumbnail. Being hosted on Pinterest does not prove an image is photographic rather than AI-generated; check visible artifacts and original sources when available, and leave unresolved provenance explicit.

Compare the strongest opening-shot pairing with a relevant benchmark before mapping the remaining locations. Keep a useful alternative if the direction is unsettled. This is internal quality control, not a mandatory additional user approval. If no candidate meets the brief, report the specific missing asset rather than presenting a weak replacement as finished.

## Candidate record

Keep stable reference IDs with:

- Pin URL and original creator/source URL if discoverable; retrieval date.
- Visual description and whether the image was actually inspected.
- Intended role: avatar styling, location, wardrobe, product, or composition.
- Matched shot IDs and specific fit: angle, depth, surfaces, light, scale, unobstructed product area.
- Conflicts: incompatible perspective, absent surfaces, excessive clutter, unwanted text or people.
- Selection judgment: benchmark fit, usable quality, creator/background composition, and concrete reasons for selection or rejection.
- Provenance/use status: inspiration only, user-supplied, licensed (record evidence), or generated.
- Performance evidence, or “unknown.” Record visible metric, platform, date, and source; keep views, saves, and paid-ad results distinct.

Do not invent URLs, creator attribution, engagement counts, or rights. A search ranking, recommendation feed, or attractive photo is not proof of virality. A successful post provides an example to study, not causal evidence that its setting drove performance.

## Reference handling

Link or display permitted previews for research. Do not bypass media display restrictions or download files merely to evade them. Download or import an image for production only when the intended use is appropriate and authorized; a pin can serve as inspiration without becoming a generation input.

Preserve the requested source mode. When asked to take images from Pinterest, the selected images themselves are the reference assets. Do not replace them with generated lookalikes, character sheets or illustrated environment panels. Keep product packaging and supplied brand assets intact. If generated assets are requested instead, use the available image-generation/editing tool and its applicable skill. If a crop or composite is useful, keep the original source visible in the review and distinguish presentation layout from the actual generation input.

Maintain one canonical avatar identity and compatible wardrobe. Additional views help only when they are demonstrably of the same identity and appropriate to the source mode; do not invent missing views automatically. Keep locations consistent across alternate angles. A source photograph shows one viewpoint: assess how much of the intended framing it actually specifies, rather than claiming it completely controls unseen geometry. Avoid conflicting or redundant references.

## Board deliverable

Use an image-led board appropriate to the task. Show source frame, selected avatar and proposed location at a useful size, with a relevant benchmark where it helps comparison. Label actual input assets separately from inspiration. A text list of pins is a fallback when previews cannot be displayed, and should be labeled as such. Include:

1. Product/category interpretation, confidence, and unresolved facts.
2. Source shot map with thumbnails when accessible.
3. A focused recommended direction and useful alternatives when a real choice remains.
4. Candidate previews, source links, use status, fit rationale, and any genuine performance evidence.
5. Recommended avatar/location IDs assigned to shot IDs.
6. Reference assets still needed and the proposed representative test.

For example, three kitchen angles may share location L01; a bedroom scene may use L02. Do not require users to upload a new background for every cut. Conversely, one unrelated room image does not precisely specify three locations.

Describe a direction as “visually promising” when performance evidence is absent. When selection is required, ask a focused question about the prepared alternatives, not a generic request for permission to research.

For revisions, first correct the actual selection, then update the board and active generation inputs. Give the artifact a new versioned filename/title and package or embed authorized preview images so the review does not depend on hotlinks. Inspect the delivered version in its viewer, including the images; filesystem contents alone do not establish what the user sees. Do not resend the same cached path as the only evidence of a correction.
